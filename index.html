Episode 1

Tooty Beetroot

and the Hunt for Butley Village-Hall

Right. Before we start. Tooty Beetroot is an explorer. He is also, as far as I can tell, Victorian. I asked him about that once. He asked if I'd had my breakfast.

I'm Wilford. I do the maps, the snacks and the bit where we don't die. This is my log. Tooty isn't allowed to read it (Tooty, if you're reading this, go away).

Friday, 7.58pm. The Swan Inn, Alderton.

Tooty doesn't walk into rooms. He bursts. Tents, shops, a lift once (the lift wasn't ready).

So when he burst into the Swan on Friday night, it went about how you'd expect. Friday is bingo night. Bingo at the Swan is silent. There's a sign over the bar that says SILENCE DURING PLAY. THIS MEANS YOU, DEREK. Nobody has seen Derek for years. They still mean him.

Forty heads turned. Forty dabbers stopped dabbing (those fat felt pens). Up on a little stage, behind a microphone, Compton Dando lifted one finger.

Compton Dando is the bingo caller. He wears a velvet jacket and smiles with only the bottom half of his face. He is also the man who has spent 3 years trying to stop Tooty Beetroot doing anything at all.

He's not a good bingo caller. Last month he called Number 12 four times, and nobody won for an hour. Nobody dares tell him.

"Eyes down," he said, very softly. "Kelly's eye. Number 1."

Tooty didn't notice. He marched straight to the bar, moustache first.

"My good fellow!" Tooty has one volume. It is Town Crier. "I am seeking a gentleman. A Mr Butley Village-Hall!"

Somebody's bingo card floated to the floor.

The landlord looked at him. He had a shaved head, a union badge, and a guitar on the wall behind him that looked like it had written an angry song about this exact situation. He leant across the bar and said, in the loudest whisper I've ever heard:

"It's bingo. Shut up."

"Splendid!" said Tooty. "I shall whisper!" (For Tooty a whisper is a shout wearing a scarf.) "Do you know the fellow? Fine family, the Village-Halls. Old money. Very upright. I knew one at school. Terrible at cricket. Sound chap."

"Legs eleven," said Compton Dando, into the microphone, very loudly, to nobody.

You know a Village-Hall. Everyone does. Sticky floor, a tea urn in the corner, a cupboard of folding chairs nobody's opened since 1987. Tooty has never been in one. He thinks it's a man. This is how most of our weeks go.

I watched the landlord work it out. You could see it going across his face, like weather. Arguing with Tooty takes 20 minutes. Giving him the answer takes 10 seconds.

"Butley Village Hall," he whispered, very slowly, "is a building. Out of here, left, past the church, second turning after the duck pond. Big ugly hut. Flagpole. That's your fella."

"A hut!" said Tooty. "Humble beginnings. I respect that." He shook the landlord's hand with both of his. "Sir, you are a credit to your parish."

In a silent pub you hear everything. Compton Dando had heard all of it. "Clickety click. Number 66," he said, and he wasn't looking at us any more. He was looking at the poster by the dartboard.

I didn't think anything of it at the time. I'd like that written down.

Saturday, 8.10am. The road out of Alderton.

We'd been going about 4 minutes when we hit the sign.

It was orange. It said DIVERSION. It had a big arrow pointing right.

"A diversion!" said Tooty, with total joy. "Wilford! In my day a diversion was a treat! A conjuror! A fair! A man with a bear!"

"It means the road's shut, Tooty."

"Then someone has gone to a great deal of trouble to entertain us."

I looked at the sign. It was a very good sign. Crisp lettering. Shiny bolts. Roadworks signs are usually a bit sad and muddy, and anyway there weren't any roadworks.

"I've got a feeling about this," I said.

"So have I!" said Tooty, and went right.

DIVERSION left. DIVERSION right. DIVERSION left again. By the third time we passed the duck pond, the duck had stopped swimming and was just watching us.

I should say that Tooty is wrong about nearly everything. He is also the only person I know who says good morning to hedges, and means it.

You can't tell Tooty he's wrong. I tried once, in 2019. He gave me a medal for Honesty and carried on. So a good navigator doesn't argue. A good navigator waits for something Tooty wants more.

"Tooty," I said. "I've heard the best diversions are the unmarked ones. The secret ones."

His big wheel stopped. "Secret diversions?"

"Second turning after the pond. No sign. Very exclusive."

"Wilford," he said, "you are a genius."

I am. But mostly I'd been listening to the landlord.

It was a muddy track between two fields. "Last time I was in Suffolk," said Tooty, "this was all fields."

"It's still all fields, Tooty."

"Then someone has preserved it magnificently."

Saturday, 8.52am. Butley Village Hall.

It was a big ugly hut with a flagpole and some bunting that had given up. Outside was a trestle table. On the table sat a wheel of cheese the size of a car tyre. Next to it stood a woman in a hi-vis vest, holding a clipboard.

No other riders. Not one. The diversions had got the lot of them. I could hear a bike bell, a long way off, going round the duck pond.

Tooty got off his penny-farthing. This takes a while. It's like climbing down a tree. Then he took off his helmet, and bowed to the building.

"Mr Village-Hall," he said. "At last. Beetroot. Tooty. Explorer. I trust I find you well."

He shook the door handle. He held on to it. For quite a long time.

Things Tooty has shaken hands with this month

  1. A vicar.
  2. The Mayor of Bedford.
  3. A gatepost.
  4. A cash machine (it gave him £20, so he thinks it likes him).

The woman with the clipboard watched all this. "You here to sign on?"

"Madam, I am here to meet the gentleman."

"Right," she said, and wrote something down. "Well, you're first. So that's the Big Cheese."

That's when I remembered the poster. FIRST RIDER TO SIGN ON. And Compton Dando, looking at it.

Tooty lifted the cheese above his head. It's 9 kilos. He isn't big. For a second he was just two legs under a cheese. "Mr Village-Hall has SURRENDERED A CHEESE!"

That's when the hedge moved.

Compton Dando came out of the hedge. He was wearing a hi-vis vest, he was covered in leaves, and he had a DIVERSION sign tucked under each arm. He'd been planning to come in the back way while we did laps of the duck. But he'd put up 7 diversions, and it turns out he'd followed one of them himself.

"That's MY cheese," he said.

"You haven't signed on, sir," said the woman, not looking up.

"I left my pen in the pub."

What had happened was this. Compton heard Tooty ask for Butley Village-Hall. He saw the poster. He put 2 and 2 together and got 5. In his head, Tooty was after the cheese.

Tooty had never heard of the cheese. Tooty still thinks the cheese was a present.

And Compton could have won it himself. He could have turned up at 8.45 and signed his name. That's all it took. No signs, no hedge. But Compton Dando has never once done a thing the easy way when a complicated way was lying around. He's very proud of this. I think it's the main reason he loses.

Tooty beamed down at him. "Dando! Thank you for the diversions. Jolly good fun. Come and meet Mr Village-Hall."

The bottom half of Compton's face smiled. "I'll get you next time, Beetroot," he said, and picked a twig out of his ear.

He says that every time. I've nearly started to like it.

Saturday, 9.05am. The rest of it.

We did the 40 miles. Tooty on the penny-farthing, me on the normal bike, the Big Cheese in my basket. It was the heaviest 40 miles of my life. (I said that last time, but that was a pumpkin.)

Saturday, 7pm. The report.

Tooty dictated this. I wrote it down.

Official report of the expedition

Mission
To locate Mr Butley Village-Hall.
Result
TRIUMPH.
Findings
Mr Village-Hall was located in the face of local hostility, a bingo evening and a bad-tempered man with a guitar. He received us warmly and surrendered a cheese.
Also
A secret diversion was discovered. The duck may be involved.
Staff
Wilford was also present.
TRIUMPH

I wrote it down exactly as he said it. I'd just like it noted that I was not "also present". I did the whole thing. But he looked so pleased.

I still don't know about the duck.

End of Episode 1
